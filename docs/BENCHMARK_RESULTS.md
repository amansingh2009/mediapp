# Baseline results — 27 October 2026

The currently active PP-OCRv5 plus regex pipeline was measured on three synthetic full-page fixtures with four labeled medicines. The previous backend and the revised numbered-row parser each found candidates on all three pages, but only **1 of 4 medicine names matched exactly**. The one exact name had the correct strength and an incorrect or missing food instruction. See the [machine-readable previous baseline](../ai/evaluation/reports/previous-ocr-synthetic.json) and [revised parser baseline](../ai/evaluation/reports/numbered-row-ocr-synthetic.json).

The parser change targets handwritten numbered lists split into separate OCR fragments or joined with adjacent text; API-level regression tests check both patterns. On a live retry of the user's saved prescription, OCR found 41 fragments, grouped them into 19 rows, and returned three **unverified** medicine-name candidates instead of zero. Those names remain uncertain and no dosage, frequency, or duration could be established. We do not know the ground-truth text of that photo. The synthetic score and live candidate count are not clinical accuracy estimates.

`ai/evaluation/benchmark.py --reader indian` is ready to run the pinned Indian Qwen candidate on the same fixtures, but its 7.52 GB weights have not been approved for download or run. No comparative accuracy result is reported and no new fine-tuning has been performed. The existing TrOCR checkpoint measured 48.25% exact words on the published RxHandBD test split, which tests isolated crops rather than complete prescriptions.
